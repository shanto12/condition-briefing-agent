# Payer Coverage Summary for Anti-Amyloid Therapies

## Traditional Medicare
- CMS covers FDA-approved anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies for Alzheimer's disease under coverage with evidence development (CED) in its national coverage determination.
- For traditional FDA approvals, coverage requires the treating clinician to take part in a qualifying registry that collects real-world outcome data.
- Our neurology group must register each treating clinician and submit registry data for every covered patient. Missing registry data puts reimbursement at risk.

## Medicare Advantage
Medicare Advantage plans must follow the national coverage policy but can add utilization management.
- Payer A (Medicare Advantage): requires prior authorization, with documented amyloid confirmation, a recent baseline MRI and APOE e4 counseling. Turnaround is 7 to 14 days.
- Payer B (Medicare Advantage): requires prior authorization plus a re-authorization every 6 months, with monitoring MRI reports attached.

## Commercial
- Payer C (commercial): covers on a medical-necessity basis with prior authorization. Its criteria mirror the Medicare registry requirements.
- Payer D (commercial): currently excludes anti-amyloid therapy pending its own policy review, which is expected in Q1 2027.

## Revenue cycle requirements
1. Build prior-authorization templates for each payer and a registry-submission workflow.
2. Add 1 authorization specialist for neurology infusion.
3. Track denials by reason code. Missing MRI documentation was the top denial reason for peer systems.

## Financial note
Drug cost is high, and reimbursement depends on clean documentation. Contracting should model 340B eligibility by site and expected denial rates before launch.
