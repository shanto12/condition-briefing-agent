# Draft Clinical Pathway: Anti-Amyloid Therapy Evaluation

## Who the pathway is for
Adults with early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease, meaning mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or mild dementia, which is the population studied for approved anti-amyloid antibodies.

## Pathway steps
1. **Clinical evaluation:** cognitive testing, staging, review of other causes of decline, and medication review. Symptomatic therapies (cholinesterase inhibitors such as donepezil, rivastigmine or galantamine, and memantine) remain standard care and continue alongside any evaluation.
2. **Confirm amyloid pathology:** amyloid PET or CSF testing. A blood biomarker (plasma p-tau217) can triage who should go on to confirmatory testing.
3. **Genetic counseling and APOE e4 testing:** labeling recommends APOE e4 testing before starting treatment to inform ARIA risk. Homozygous carriers have the highest risk.
4. **Baseline MRI:** a recent brain MRI to identify findings that raise ARIA risk, such as multiple microhemorrhages or superficial siderosis.
5. **Medication safety review:** anticoagulant use needs a documented risk discussion, because of bleeding concerns.
6. **Shared decision-making:** discuss expected benefit, infusion schedule, monitoring burden and ARIA risk with the patient and care partner.
7. **Treatment and monitoring:** infusion schedule per product label, safety MRIs at labeled intervals, and a hold-and-review protocol when ARIA is detected.

## Exclusions and referral-out criteria (planning summary)
Moderate or severe dementia, no amyloid confirmation, contraindicated MRI findings, or inability to complete monitoring.

## Staffing implications
Each treated patient needs a nurse navigator to coordinate testing, authorization, infusions and MRIs. Plan for 1 navigator per 60 to 70 active patients.
