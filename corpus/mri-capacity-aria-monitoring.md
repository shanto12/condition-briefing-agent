# MRI Capacity and ARIA Safety Monitoring Plan

## Why this matters
Anti-amyloid antibodies carry a boxed warning for amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA): brain swelling (ARIA-E) or small bleeds (ARIA-H). Product labels call for a recent baseline brain MRI before treatment starts and scheduled safety MRIs during the first months of treatment, plus extra scans if symptoms appear. Radiologists need training to recognize and grade ARIA.

## Current capacity
- 9 MRI scanners across the system, 3 of them 3-Tesla units suited to neuro protocols.
- The average wait for a routine outpatient neuro MRI is 18 days.
- Neuro MRI read turnaround averages 31 hours.

## Added demand from an anti-amyloid program (year one, internal estimate)
- 140 baseline MRIs for treated patients, plus about 60 for patients screened but not treated.
- About 4.5 safety-monitoring MRIs per treated patient in year one: roughly 630 scans.
- About 50 unscheduled symptom-driven scans.
- Total: about 880 additional MRIs in year one, or 2.2% more neuro MRI volume.

## Requirements
1. **Protected slots:** reserve 6 protected 3T slots per week for ARIA-protocol scans, so safety MRIs happen before the next scheduled infusion.
2. **Fast reads:** target read turnaround of 24 hours for ARIA-protocol scans. Results go to the infusion team before the next dose.
3. **Standard protocol:** use a standardized ARIA protocol including susceptibility-weighted imaging (SWI) and FLAIR sequences.
4. **Training:** two neuroradiologists complete ARIA grading training by the end of Q4 2026.

## Risk
Without protected slots, MRI waits could delay infusions and push treated patients out of the label's monitoring windows.
