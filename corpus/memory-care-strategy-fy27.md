# Memory Care Service Line Strategy Memo, FY2027

## Purpose
This memo asks the Executive Planning Committee to choose how Northlake Health responds to growing demand for dementia diagnosis and to the arrival of anti-amyloid therapies for early Alzheimer's disease.

## Situation
- New memory clinic referrals reached 2,430 in FY2026, up 22% year over year.
- The median wait for a new memory clinic visit is 94 days, well above our 30-day target.
- About 140 patients per year could be eligible for anti-amyloid therapy once screening, amyloid confirmation and MRI safety checks are complete.
- Our infusion centers run at 86% utilization, and neuro MRI waits average 18 days.
- Lakeshore Neuro Institute, our main academic competitor, launched an anti-amyloid infusion program in 2024.

## Options compared
**Option A: launch an anti-amyloid program at West Campus.** Dedicate 3 infusion chairs, add protected MRI slots for safety monitoring, and hire 2 neurologists, 1 neuropsychologist and 2 nurse navigators. Estimated capital and start-up cost: $1.8M. This keeps patients in our system and supports future trial participation.

**Option B: partner with Lakeshore Neuro Institute for infusion.** Northlake keeps diagnosis and follow-up, and Lakeshore delivers infusions. Low capital need, but we lose infusion revenue, and patients face a longer drive.

**Option C: defer 12 months.** Watch real-world safety data and payer policy. Lowest cost, but referral leakage to competitors is likely to grow.

## Recommendation
Option A, with three conditions:
1. Fix the diagnostic bottleneck first: neuropsychology backlog and blood biomarker triage.
2. Confirm MRI read turnaround for safety-monitoring scans.
3. Apply to the CMS GUIDE Model to fund caregiver support.

## Decision needed
Approval to proceed with Option A planning, with a go/no-go review at the end of Q2 FY2027.
